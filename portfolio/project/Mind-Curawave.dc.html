Curawave: A Wave-Bionic Soft Robotic Mattress for Post-Operative Patient Transfer
Design question. Can a mattress slide a patient sideways onto another bed the way a wave moves a boat, safely and without anyone lifting?
Amy Hu, CTO · with Emily Feng (CEO) and Galatea Shang (CFO)
As CTO and founder
I led the technology: mould design, silicone casting, valves and Arduino control. My startup, Beijing Lingtong Future Technology, raised over USD 100k to develop assistive products (p. 20).
Why I built it
The idea began with my own bicycle accident. With an injured leg I struggled to move from place to place, and every attempt was shocked by pain. Moving patients after surgery is the same problem at hospital scale: in 2024 China had about 70 million people bedridden by injury and 96.4 million surgical operations.

Summary  Among 1,200 post-operative patients in a study we cited, 15% experienced complications related to being transferred, and different moving devices and transition times influence secondary injury. Curawave is a mattress that imitates the movement of ocean waves to transfer patients safely and comfortably without human labour. It is built from 18 × 20 small soft units, each with a hollow silicone inflation layer, a non-stretchable cotton confining layer and a silicone base. Because the confining layer stretches less than the inflation layer, each unit tilts sideways, left or right, when inflated; adjacent units are grouped and inflated alternately, so a sideways wave slides the patient horizontally off one bed and onto the next, with no one lifting. We modelled the moulds in Autodesk Fusion, 3D-printed them, cast the silicone units, and drove an air pump through solenoid valves controlled by an Arduino, producing a working 1 × 4 prototype.
Market & plan
China's medical-device market is about 120 billion yuan. We start with first-tier hospitals: direct sales, training videos and yearly maintenance. Forecast turnover grows from ¥2.3M to ¥21.6M in five years, with 1% to charity.
Next steps
AI that reads the forces on a patient and adjusts each unit's inflation; a patent on how the units are assembled to mimic waves; and markets beyond mainland China.
