Polarisation Control and Experimental Optics
Aim. Learn to measure light precisely, then control its colour, intensity and polarisation in a research lab.
Amy Hu · nonlinear optics research intern · 12 written lab reports
Summary  I worked through twelve classic optics experiments, from the focal length of a thin lens to static laser holography, and wrote each up as a full lab report with aims, principle, procedure, data and error analysis. The measurements agree closely with reference values: a He-Ne wavelength of about 633 nm against 632.8 nm, the mercury green line at about 546 nm against 546.1 nm, and a normalised intensity of 0.502 at 45° against the cos²θ prediction of 0.500. In the research lab I then operated graduate-level Raman and laser systems, tested more than twenty polarisation states of the beam, and spoke with a Nobel laureate about where experimental physics is heading.

1 · Geometric optics

With the conjugate method the focal length of a convex lens came out at 100.0 mm against a nominal 100 mm; the method only needs the screen distance D and the lens shift d, f = (D² − d²)/4D, which cancels the uncertain position of the lens's optical centre. A calibrated eyepiece micrometer gave 8.40 µm per division and a fibre diameter of 71 µm. At minimum deviation, a glass prism gave n ≈ 1.517 and showed dispersion: violet bends most, red least.

2 · Interference

In Newton's rings the air film between a lens and a flat plate produces dark rings with Dm² = 4mRλ. Using two widely separated orders removes the uncertain contact point, and gave R ≈ 0.85 m.

An air wedge gave a sheet thickness of about 74 µm by measuring 20 fringe spacings at once, and Young's double slit gave a He-Ne wavelength of about 633 nm.

3 · Diffraction

Single-slit Fraunhofer diffraction gave a slit width of 0.20 mm and confirmed that the pattern widens as the slit narrows. A transmission grating, read on both sides of zero order to cancel offset error, gave the mercury green line at about 546 nm.

4 · Polarisation

Rotating an analyser in 15° steps reproduced Malus's law, I = I0cos²θ, almost exactly; the small residual intensity at 90° comes from the finite extinction ratio of real polarisers.
