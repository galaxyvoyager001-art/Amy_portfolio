Electrical Damping
Problem. A magnet suspended by a spring will display simple harmonic motion when displaced. If the magnet oscillates within a coil connected to a resistor, its motion will be damped. Investigate the factors that affect the damping.
1 · The physics

A magnet hanging on a spring oscillates inside a coil wired to a resistor. As it moves, the changing flux induces an emf; the induced current feels an Ampère force that pushes back on the magnet, and its energy leaves as Joule heat in the resistor. Damping is therefore a chain: mechanical energy → electrical energy → heat. In a preliminary run (R = 10 Ω, N = 400 turns, μ = 27.02 A·m²) the amplitude decayed exponentially, and damping was slightly stronger when the magnet was near the coil's edge.

2 · Model

I modelled the bar magnet as an equivalent current sheet (measured magnetisation M = (1.12 ± 0.04) × 106 A/m) and integrated Biot–Savart over the coil to get the flux ψ(x, N, B) and the mutual-inductance gradient G = dψ/dx, so ε = G ẋ. Kirchhoff and Newton then couple the circuit to the motion:

G ẋ = L İ + I R
m ẍ = −k(x − x0) − mg + I G − 2mγmechẋ
ignoring inductance: Fem = −(G²/R) ẋ,  γem = G²/R

Solved with RK4, the motion is underdamped; an FFT of the current shows several superposed frequencies, because the magnet's field has strong nonlinear terms.

3 · Predictions

Turns: damping rises quickly up to a peak at 2,700 ± 10 turns, then slowly falls to 6,000 turns as the coil's own inductive reactance chokes the current. Resistance: damping falls roughly as 1/R. Magnetic moment: since ε ∝ m and F ∝ I·m, damping first grows quadratically, then levels off as the heavier magnet gains inertia.

4 · Experiment

Spring 16 N/m; total mass 0.05 kg; N52 cylinder magnet 8 mm × 12 mm; coil 2,666.67 turns/m. I varied turns (200, 800, 1,600), magnets (2–16) and resistance (2–10 Ω), tracked the motion on video with Tracker and logged current. With the circuit open, mechanical damping was γmech = 0.0162 ± 0.0005; at Re ≈ 183 air drag is linear, so it could be subtracted cleanly.

5 · Results

All three trends match the model. Errors: a 3.6% magnetisation error doubles to 7.2% in damping (F ∝ M²); inductance carries 10%; large amplitudes push the spring past linearity, and a slight swing or spin of the magnet changes dψ/dt.

Levitation Control
Problem. When arranged in a specific configuration, small graphite sheets can levitate on neodymium magnets. By shining light onto the surface of the graphite sheet, it is possible to control its movement. Explain and investigate the phenomenon.
1 · The physics

Pyrolytic graphite is diamagnetic, so it floats above the potential minimum of a checkerboard of NdFeB magnets. A laser spot heats one edge; graphite's susceptibility depends on temperature, so the force on the sheet becomes uneven and it tilts and slides. Depending on laser power and speed, it stays levitated, relaxes back, or follows the light continuously.

2 · Field and force

For an m × n array of alternating cubic magnets I built the field from surface currents (J = 3.651 × 105 A/m²) by Biot–Savart and superposition. With anisotropic χ (χ∥, χ⊥) and χ ≪ 1, the energy density is u = −χB²/2μ0. Levitation balances ρg = (χ⊥/μ0) Bz ∂Bz/∂z; the periodic array term cancels over a cycle, leaving the light-driven term:

Fr = (h / 2μ0) · (dχ∥/dT) · (dT/dr) · ∬ Bxy² dA

From the Landau free energy, χ depends on temperature; I used the measured χ⊥ = (−743 + T) × 10−6 and χ∥ = (−135 + 0.25T) × 10−6.

3 · Temperature field

A 3-D transient heat equation with a Gaussian spot, solved by Hankel and Laplace transforms, gives the surface temperature. At 300 mW the gradient is constant from 0.05 s on, with ΔT = 1.36 K across the sheet; a moving frame X = x − vxt handles a scanning beam.

4 · Motion

Newton's law m r̈ = Fr − Fk − ζṙ combines light drive, a restoring force from the array's potential (periodic with the magnet edge l) and thin-film air drag. If Fr ≤ Fk,max + drag the sheet relaxes within one period l; above it, it moves continuously. A torque equation I θ̈ = τlight + τm − βθ̇ adds the tilt.

5 · Experiment, honestly

Magnets of 10 and 5 mm at 0.1–0.4 T, graphite 0.3 mm and 50 µm, a 0–1.6 W laser on a 2-D gantry and a thermal camera. The sheet levitated and heated as predicted, but my set-up did not yet reproduce light-driven travel. My analysis: N52 magnets hold the sheet too firmly for a 1.6 W beam. Next: weaker magnets, thinner graphite, more power.
