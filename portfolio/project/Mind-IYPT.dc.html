Energy Transport and Localization in a Chain of Magnetically Coupled Pendulums
Research question. How do relevant parameters affect the normal-mode frequencies and thereby influence energy transport and localization in a chain of magnetically coupled pendulums?
AMY HU · INDEPENDENT RESEARCH · IYPT MACAO TEAM CAPTAIN
Abstract  A chain of magnetically coupled pendulums is a macroscopic analogue of systems in which interacting particles exchange and transport vibrational energy: the pendulums are discrete sites, gravity provides an on-site potential, and magnetic repulsion is the nonlinear interaction between neighbours. I measured the normal-mode frequencies of chains of two to five pendulums while varying spacing, magnetic moment, pendulum length and release angle, and compared them with a Lagrangian model built from independently measured parameters. The model reproduced the measured frequencies to within a few percent (mean absolute difference ≈ 1.37%, RMSE 0.045 Hz at θ0 = 30.3°). The nearly in-phase mode remains controlled by gravity, while higher modes rise with magnetic moment and fall strongly with spacing; larger release angles produce amplitude-dependent softening. Used as a numerical tool, the model shows how the spectrum decides whether energy spreads through the chain, returns to a few modes, or stays localised.

1 · Motivation

Unlike microscopic systems, the displacement of every pendulum can be observed directly, while the coupling strength, lattice spacing, damping and initial excitation can be controlled experimentally. The chain lets dispersive wave propagation, modal energy exchange, recurrence and energy localization be studied in an accessible, measurable form.

2 · Background

Magnetic repulsion is a contactless, adjustable realisation of a nonlinear oscillator lattice; attached to pendulums, gravity adds an on-site potential and a lower cutoff frequency. Moving breathers have been observed in a ring of magnetic pendulums, and the Fermi–Pasta–Ulam problem showed that energy placed in a few modes may return instead of spreading. The connection between adjustable parameters, the complete eigenfrequency spectrum of a finite chain, and the resulting transport of energy remained insufficiently established.

3 · Hypothesis

Stronger magnetic coupling, from larger magnetisation or smaller spacing, should widen the spectrum and raise most the modes with large relative motion between neighbours. The nearly in-phase mode should stay governed by the gravitational torque.

4 · Method

The apparatus used a rigid top rail, sliding PETG brackets, bearing-mounted arms and magnets held in baskets, with a camera normal to the plane of motion. For each setting one end pendulum was released from θ0, recorded at 30 frames s−1 and repeated at least three times; only one variable was changed per series. Each bob was tracked in Tracker, and the eigenfrequencies were extracted by discrete Fourier transform, accepting a peak as a collective mode only when it appeared in more than one pendulum with compatible phases.

The model is a Lagrangian with Rayleigh dissipation: Ieq θ̈n + ∂V/∂θn + γ θ̇n = 0, with gravity and the measured magnetic pair potential in V.

5 · Results

At θ0 = 30.3° the measured frequencies were 1.432, 2.546 and 3.952 Hz, against model values of 1.425, 2.492 and 3.896 Hz: a mean absolute difference of about 1.37% and an RMSE of 0.045 Hz. For N = 3, 4 and 5 the discrepancies were 2.09%, 2.50% and 6.09%, growing with long-range coupling and magnet heterogeneity.

Higher modes rose with magnetic moment and fell strongly with spacing; longer pendulums lowered all frequencies; larger release angles produced amplitude-dependent softening.

6 · Conclusion

Spacing, magnetic moment, length, release angle, chain size and damping first set the mode frequencies, bandwidth and nonlinear detuning, which then decide whether energy propagates through several sites, exchanges among a few modes, or becomes spatially confined. This sequence is supported experimentally for the spectrum and numerically for transport and localization.
