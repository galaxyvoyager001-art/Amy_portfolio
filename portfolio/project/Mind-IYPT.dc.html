A Cradle Without CollisionsExtended Essay · CYPT 2026

In a Newton's cradle, energy passes from ball to ball by collision. Replace the balls with repelling magnets and the energy still travels, but now without contact, the way atoms in a crystal trade energy through forces. That makes a desktop cradle a working model of a lattice.

Method

I designed and 3D-printed the rig, then varied three parameters: magnet strength, the spacing between pendulums and the release angle. Every trial was filmed and tracked frame by frame; I extracted each pendulum's eigenfrequencies with a fast Fourier transform and compared them with a Lagrangian model of repelling dipoles with Rayleigh damping.

Findings

The motion is a mix of normal modes: the first frequency is set mainly by gravity, the second by the magnetic coupling. The release angle decides which frequency dominates, while spacing and magnet strength tune the second. Model and experiment agree within about 3%, and the chain is non- or only weakly chaotic, so energy can be followed as it moves or stays localised.

From tournament to essay

The question began as an IYPT problem and became my Extended Essay. At CYPT 2026 I reported two more problems with the same method: draw the set-up, reduce it to one governing equation, then test that equation on my own apparatus.

In Electrical Damping, damping rises with coil turns, peaks near 2,700 turns and then falls as the coil's own inductance takes over; it falls as 1/R. In Levitation Control, light heats one corner of floating graphite, its susceptibility drops, and the sheet is pushed away from the light; my simulation gives a 1.36 K difference across the sheet.
