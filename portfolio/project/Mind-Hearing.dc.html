SmartHearing: Scene-Adaptive Hearing Assistance via LALM-Guided Language-Queried Sound Enhancement
Research question. Which sound matters most right now, and how can a hearing aid enhance it in a scene it has never heard before?
Xiangyu Kong & Amy (Wanying) Hu, equal contribution · ICASSP 2027 submission
Abstract  Conventional hearing aids prioritize speech, yet which sound matters most (a siren, an alarm, a scream) depends on the acoustic scene. Speech-centric models suppress non-speech warnings, and scene classifiers rely on predefined labels that do not transfer to unseen environments. We propose a scene-adaptive hearing assistance framework that couples a large audio language model (LALM) for open-vocabulary scene understanding with language-queried sound separation. A lightweight Query-space Adapter bridges the representation shift between LALM-generated descriptions and the separator's training queries, and an adaptive window selects 4–10 s of context per sound category. On 600 mixtures, the Adapter improves SNRi and SI-SDRi by 0.97 dB and 1.14 dB over the base separator; on 145 unseen ESC-50 mixtures, the gains persist (+1.32 dB, +1.19 dB). In a 101-participant listening test, the system was rated most salient in 50.5% of trials versus 28.1% and 21.5% for baselines.

1 · Motivation

Hearing-impaired listeners regularly meet complex acoustic environments, from restaurants and shopping malls to multi-speaker meetings, where background noise, mixed sounds and overlapping speech coexist. Conventional hearing aids primarily amplify human voices, so other sounds that carry critical information, such as a car horn on the street, may be attenuated, creating fatal risk.

2 · Background

Acoustic scene classification assigns one predefined label, such as "office", and generalizes poorly to unseen or mixed scenes; speech enhancement treats the human voice as the only target. Large audio language models now show strong audio understanding and causal reasoning. To our knowledge, this work is the first to combine LALM-based open-vocabulary scene reasoning with language-queried source separation (LASS) for scene-adaptive hearing assistance.

3 · Method

SmartHearing has a perception path and an execution path that run asynchronously, so inference never blocks audio. The perception path uses Qwen3.5-Omni to decompose each window into sound objects ok = (sound, role, danger, confidence, relevance), with six roles from urgent warning to unwanted noise.

A frozen CLAP encoder maps each target description to an embedding, and the Query-space Adapter applies a residual correction, e′k = ek + αkAθ(ek). LASS then extracts each source, and an Audio Processor assigns gains scaled by risk and confidence: yt = gbbt + Σ gksk.

4 · Experiments

Adapter V1 is trained on FSD50K; V2 adds ESC-50, UrbanSound8K, NIGENS and TUT Rare Sound Events 2017. Each of seven target classes contributes 500 training and 100 validation mixtures, with ten interferers and SNRs from −10 to 10 dB. Baselines include a Wiener filter, GTCRN and the base LASS separator.

5 · Results

Speech denoisers collapse on non-speech targets, and a fixed query collapses entirely. SmartHearing reaches 11.24 dB SNRi, close to the oracle upper bound that is given the true target.

6 · Conclusion

A proper Adapter between the perception and execution paths improves the separation quality of target sounds. Future work will cross-check LALM outputs to raise decision confidence and add evaluation with hearing-impaired listeners.
