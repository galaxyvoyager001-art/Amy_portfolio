METAL
Micro-particle impacts on metallic glass at high temperature.
Spacecraft shields are hit by tiny particles at enormous speed, often while hot. Crystalline metals can get stronger under fast impact as temperature rises; ordinary glasses get softer. Metallic glass is both a metal and a glass, so which way does it go?
SamplePd₄₃Ni₁₀Cu₂₇P₂₀ bulk metallic glass, confirmed amorphous by XRD and DSC
ImpactLaser-induced particle impact: 30 µm silica spheres at 70–700 m/s, filmed by a high-speed camera
Heat25, 100 and 200 °C, held by a PID controller
ModelFinite-element simulation with a shear-transformation constitutive model

At 200 °C the glass flows: craters turn fluid-like and dynamic yield strength drops about 10.4%, from 2089 to 1825 MPa.
Why it matters
Temperature decides whether this material resists or flows. Choosing a shield material by its room-temperature strength alone could be a mistake in space.
