Flowedmetal

Micro-particle impacts on metallic glasses under high temperature.
RoleFirst author
LabInstitute of Mechanics, CAS
SupervisorProf. Minqiang Jiang
RecognitionISEF Beijing qualifier
( Overview )

Glass shatters; metal bends. A metallic glass is both. I fired 30 µm particles at hundreds of metres per second into a Pd-based metallic glass at 25, 100 and 200 °C to find out whether heat makes it harder, like a metal, or softer, like a glass.

−0%dynamic yield strength, 25 → 200 °C
0 M fpshigh-speed camera
0test temperatures
0+impact tests

01The experiment

A 7 ns, 532 nm laser pulse ablates a gold film and launches a silica particle at 1–1000 m/s; a camera at up to five million frames per second records the impact and rebound. A PID controller holds the target within ±1 K. I prepared and characterised every sample, ran every impact and analysed the craters by SEM and confocal microscopy.

02Solid at 25 °C, fluid at 200 °C

At high temperature the glass dissipates more energy, its dynamic yield strength falls by 10.4%, and the crater rim splashes outward like a viscous fluid.

03Why: shear transformations

I built a quarter-symmetric ABAQUS model with 1.35 million elements and a coupled shear-transformation / free-volume law. It matched the measured crater and showed why: at 200 °C contact lasts longer, stress is lower and many more shear transformations activate.
