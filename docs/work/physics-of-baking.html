The physicsof baking

Twelve months of recipes, science and self. Written, photographed, designed and published by me.
Format38-page paperback
PublishedAmazon KDP
RolesBaker, photographer, writer, designer

( Overview )

Each month pairs one recipe with a page on the physics that makes it work: how whipped egg whites hold air, why folded butter turns into flaky layers, when a crust begins to brown.

01What happens as the temperature rises

The science pages follow the oven from cold butter to caramel.

34 °CButter melts
62 °CEgg whites set: proteins unfold and bond
70 °CStarch gelatinises
100 °CWater turns to steam, ~1,700× the volume: oven spring
150 °CMaillard browning
170 °CCaramelisation: sugar itself breaks down

02One year, twelve experiments

Foams, emulsions, heat transfer, starch and fat: twelve ways physics ends up on a plate.

03A page, opened

February is Patience: croissants, and the physics of lamination. Butter and dough are folded again and again until hundreds of thin layers alternate; in the oven, water in the butter turns to steam and pushes the layers apart.
