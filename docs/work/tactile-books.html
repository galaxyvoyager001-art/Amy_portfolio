01 · Touch · Care & Reading

Even withoutsight

Tactile picture books in felt and braille for blind children, and the visits, exhibition and film around them.
RoleActivities leader & organiser
ClubCare & Reading, 100+ members
PartnerABF, a Beijing NGO for visually impaired children
Output3 books · 10+ visits · exhibition · short film
( Overview )

Every week, each volunteer in our club tutors one blind child online, and the same pairs stay together year after year. I led 30+ members to design and hand-sew tactile picture books in felt and braille, so the stories could be read with fingertips.

01The Kingdom of Shapes

Each book is a story set in the Kingdom of Shapes. In the first, Jianjian the triangle crosses town to a New Year concert, and every scene hides a triangle to find by touch. In the second, Fangfang the square turns into something new on every page: a flying carpet, a car, a drum, a gem.

Each spread pairs a felt scene with a braille story card. A raised-line icon on the card tells the child which shape to find next.

02Drawn before it was sewn

Before any felt was cut, I drew every spread in pencil: the layout, the loose parts, the joints in section and a cutting list.

03How a page is made

Everything for one page was packed into a kit, so every club member could make a spread at home.

01Measure & markShapes are ruled onto the felt before anything is cut.
02Cut the feltEach scene piece is cut by hand.
03Sew the stripsFelt strips are stitched down to make edges a finger can trace.
04Fix the braille cardThe story card is fixed opposite the scene.

04Rules I designed by

Every shape is raised in felt strips, metal or stitched edges. Something on each page spins, rings or lights up. Nothing is loose: thread ends are covered and small parts are sewn or sealed.

05The visits

We have visited the children more than ten times. Each visit starts with ice-breaker games, then a workshop such as My Little Planet, where children paint with materials they can feel. We play, read the books together and interview the NGO teachers, and we filmed one visit as a short documentary.

Because each child keeps the same volunteer week after week, the reading continues long after the visit ends.

"Let the warmth of your fingertips become the light that helps blind children perceive the world."Exhibition poster

06The exhibition

We closed the project with a club exhibition: five hands-on stations on braille, the shoes and canes blind people use, facts about blindness, our documentary and a table of tactile materials. More than 60 visitors came over a week.
